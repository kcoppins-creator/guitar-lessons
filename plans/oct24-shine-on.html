  • Standard. Prefer neck PU, delay, sustain. Section focus: famous 4-note motif / early solo phrases (per lesson).

Pocket cue: Delay time ≈ dotted feel under a slow pulse (~60–66). Play the motif; wait until you hear the echo answer once before the next phrase. If the room fills with notes, you skipped the conversation.

2. Key & context

Common analysis: G minor (G–B♭–D) with lifts toward B♭ major. Confirm exact section key with your lesson; write concert pitch in notes.

Two cities, one metro: G minor and B♭ major are relatives — same note pool, different home stop. When the harmony brightens to B♭ (III), you’ve taken the train downtown; when you’re on Gm, you’re on the quiet side street. Motif works in both neighborhoods.

5. Exercises

  1. Motif ×20 slowly with delay on. After each, wait for one echo.
  2. Spell Gm 1–3–5 under motif — “G, B♭ (m3), D.”
  3. Roman flash: Point at Gm / Cm / D7 / B♭ and say i / iv / V7 / III.
  4. Learn phrase 1 (first 4–8 bars of solo section in lesson) at 50% speed. Mark which notes are chord tones.
  5. Call-response: play motif, invent answering phrase using only Gm or D7 chord tones.
  6. Nearest-inversion path ×4: Gm→Cm→D→Gm on strings 2–3–4 (furniture rule).
Toolbox: The motif is one wrench you already love. Tonight’s job is knowing which bolt (chord tone) each note of the motif sits on — so when you improvise the answer phrase, you’re not guessing.
