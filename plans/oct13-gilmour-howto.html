How to play like Gilmour — phrasing lab

Tue Oct 20 · 6:00–6:30 PM ET 30 min Gilmour / Floyd

Coach: Bends are already a strength (4/5) — use them as targets, not decoration. Weak links tonight: space (pocket 2/5) and landing on named chord tones (1–3–5 callouts 1/5). Shine On is Sat Oct 24 — this lab feeds that. Keep Time Guru on speaker/wired (not AirPods) for the space drill.

1. Setup

  • Tuning: Standard. Neck pickup or rolled-off bridge; mild drive + delay if available.
  • Backing: slow Am–G–F–E or Em–C–D vamp (phone looper / YouTube backing — any slow minor vamp).
Say out loud: “Tonight is phrasing, not notes-per-second. Bend to pitch, leave space, vibrato last. Over Am I aim A–C–E — C is the m3 cry.”
Pocket cue (Time Guru): Today’s rung = click on 2 and 4 (hold here; space is the skill) — in a count of 1-2-3-4, the app clicks only on beats 2 and 4 (the backbeat, where a snare usually hits); you play through all four beats. Slow tempo. Use speaker or wired headphones — not AirPods. Groove target: one Gilmour phrase + rest over your Am–G–F–E (or Em) vamp. After every phrase, count “2-3-4” out loud before the next note — if you can’t hear the delay trail die, you started too soon. Foot tap on quarters (every count: 1, 2, 3, and 4), even when the click is only on 2&4. Optional gap/random mute once (app silences the click on purpose so you hear if you rushed when it returns). Voice Memo: record one pass, then listen once — check if you sped up (~6–8 min).

2. Key & context

Lab key suggestion: A minor (A–C–E) with neighboring G major (G–B–D) and F (F–A–C) — classic Floyd-adjacent colors. Or use Bm if that sits better for your bends.

No single “song key” — transfer these tools to Shine On (Sat Oct 24 + Tue Nov 3).

Conversation, not a speech: A Gilmour phrase is one sentence. The rest is the other person nodding. If you monologue for eight bars, it’s not Floyd — it’s a scale exam.

5. Exercises

  1. Bend lab ×10: Target C (m3 of Am) or E (5). Bend from a whole step below; hold 2 beats with vibrato. Say the degree on arrival.
  2. Space drill: Play 3 notes, rest 2 beats (count aloud), repeat — mute the tendency to fill.
  3. Copy 1 phrase from Signals or Guitarist Gilmour lesson (watch ≤3 min), then invent an answer phrase using only chord tones of the current chord.
  4. Over vamp ×4: Am–G–F–E; resolve every 4th bar to Am root or b3. On E7, aim G♯ (M3) then resolve to A.
  5. Nearest-inversion sketch (if time): Am→G→F on strings 2–3–4 with common tones.
Toolbox: Minor pentatonic is the drawer. Triad tones are the three tools Gilmour actually finishes sentences with. Scales fill practice; chord tones finish songs.
