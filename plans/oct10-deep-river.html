2. Key & context

Concert: E (major blues / E7 tonal center). Capo 1 → sounding F with same shapes.

Classic Doc Watson fingerstyle: boom-chuck / alternating bass under melody. Progression is a 16-bar blues cousin, not strict 12-bar.

Toolbox: A major triad is three wrenches (1–3–5). A dominant 7th adds the b7 wrench — same bolt, extra leverage toward the next chord. Blues lives in that “almost resolved” feel.

5. Exercises

  1. 7th spell lock: E7 arpeggio — say “E, G♯ (M3), B (P5), D (m7 / b7).” Same for A7 and B7 once each.
  2. Grip cycle ×8: E7 → Edim7 → E7 → A7 (one bar each). Say Romans: I7 – I° – I7 – IV7.
  3. Thumb only ×4: Alternating bass on E7 for 8 bars — no melody. Metronome on; if you rush, drop to 66 BPM.
  4. Add fingers ×8: Bring chord tones / melody on top; mute video after one phrase. Thumb tempo stays sacred.
  5. Turnaround ×6: …E → B7 → E. Feel the V7 pull (red → green).
  6. Triad awareness: On strings 2–3–4, outline E major (E–G♯–B) vs flatten to dim color for 2 minutes — call 1–3–5.
