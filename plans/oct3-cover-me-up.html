Cover Me Up — key / chords / targets

Sat Oct 3 · 7:00–8:15 AM ET 75 min Isbell / Americana Jason Isbell

Coach (from your placement): You already can play Cover Me Up — today we attach names to what your hands know. Say interval names (M3 / m3 / P5), not only note letters. Call 1–3–5 out loud on every grip. Romans are the street signs; chord shapes are the houses.

1. Setup (5 min)

  • Tuning: Drop D (low E → D). Tune the open 6th string down a whole step; check octave with open 4th (both D).
  • Capo: Fret 2 (matches Isbell recording / common live — concert E). Marty Music teaches capo 1 (concert E♭) with the same shapes — either works; pick one and stay there all session.
  • Gear: Acoustic preferred (or clean electric). Phone ready for exit clip.
  • Metronome feel: ~72–80 BPM, gentle 4/4 country-rock ballad. No rush.
Say out loud: “Shapes are in D (Drop D + capo). Concert key with capo 2 is E major. I–IV–vi–ii in E: E, A, C♯m, F♯m.”
Pocket cue: Place the metronome on beats 2 & 4 (backbeat). Sit behind the click by a hair — Isbell pocket is a hammock, not a trampoline. If you rush the walkdown into Bm, you’re early.

2. Key & context

Guitar-relative (shapes above capo): D major family — open D shapes in Drop D.

Concert pitch:

  • Capo 2 → sounding E major (recommended for this plan).
  • Capo 1 (Marty) → sounding E♭ major.
  • No capo Drop D → sounding D major (darker; fine for practice).

All frets below are relative to the capo (capo = “nut”).

GPS / map: Capo + Drop D is like setting your GPS to “shapes in D.” Romans (I–IV–vi–ii) are the destination names in the real world (concert E). Same trip; two map layers.

Interval naming drill (placement gap)

On open D shape (concert E): root→3rd = M3 (E→G♯). On Bm (C♯m): root→b3 = m3 (C♯→E). Say the name, then the notes.

Traffic lights: I (E) = green — home. IV (A) = yellow — leaving. vi (C♯m) = soft side street. The D/C♯ walkdown is the blinker before you turn into Bm.

Form sketch (common lesson version)

  • Intro / verse feel: D – G – D (with riff on D), then walkdown D → D/C♯ → Bm → G → D.
  • Chorus: D – G – D … then Em – G – Bm – D (“cover me up…”).
  • Same shapes throughout — dynamics and riff density change, not the harmony.

Intro riff skeleton (relative frets — Marty / common tabs)

From a held D shape:

  1. G string: 2 h 4 then back to 2 (melody hammer).
  2. D string walk: 4 – 2 – 0 while low D may ring.
  3. Land G: low strings at fret 5, then often 5–4 on A string into open D / full D.

Work it slowly with Marty muted after one watch-pass — don’t binge the video.

5. Exercises (25 + 15 min)

  1. Chord change loop (4 passes) — Strum D–G–D | D–G–D | D/C♯–Bm–G–D. Whole notes then half notes. On each change say: Roman + 1–3–5 (e.g. “vi — C♯ E G♯”).
  2. Interval name pass (2) — Same loop; only say M3 or m3 for the quality of the 3rd as you land.
  3. Intro riff ×8 — Hands only, no singing. Tempo ~70. Stop if the hammer or 4–2–0 muddies; isolate 2 bars. Click on 2 & 4.
  4. Verse vocal-sim strum — Drop the fancy riff; plain D↔G with the A-string hammer embellishment Marty shows (open A → 2). 2 full verse cycles.
  5. Chorus insert — Em–G–Bm–D twice. Keep Drop-D Em grip honest (fret 2 on low three).
  6. Triad drill A — strings 2–3–4 (4 passes): nearest-inversion path for D→G→Bm→D. Rule: move ≤2 frets when possible; keep common tones. Furniture: If a note is already in the next chord, leave that finger sit — don’t rearrange the whole room.
  7. Triad drill B — strings 1–2–3 (4 passes): same progression, top set. Say “1–3–5” of each chord as you land.
  8. Target-tone call — Over a looped D–G–Bm–D, play only roots for 1 pass, only 3rds for 1 pass, only 5ths for 1 pass.
Toolbox: Full open chords = whole toolbox on the table. Triads on a string set = the three wrenches that fit this bolt. Scales can wait — tighten the chord tones first.

8. Exit ticket

Done means:
  • You said the key correctly (shapes in D / concert E with capo 2) and named at least one M3 and one m3 by interval name.
  • You can fret D, G, Bm, Em, D/C♯ from memory in Drop D and call 1–3–5 on Bm/C♯m.
  • Record a 60s phone clip: intro riff → into verse D–G loop (sing or hum optional). One take; keep the mistake if groove holds. Prefer click on 2 & 4 audible in the room.
